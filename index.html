Deciphering Earth's Climate by Sound

NASA collects petabytes of Earth observation data every day. While 2.2 billion visually impaired individuals cannot inspect satellite heatmaps and visual chart inspection induces fatigue, human hearing offers superior temporal resolution. Earth Jukebox translates multi-decadal satellite observations into continuous physical sound.

Core Mission Capabilities
1. BVI Earth Science Accessibility

Enables blind and low-vision researchers and students to explore NASA GIS datasets through tactile pitch intervals and acoustic cursors.

Acoustic Mapping Architecture
Value → Pitch

Monotonic linear mapping from data minimum to maximum across MIDI 48 (C3, 130.8 Hz) through MIDI 84 (C6, 1046.5 Hz).

Variability → Timbre

Uniform spatial data renders as a pure sinusoidal tone; elevated spatial variance introduces rich harmonic sawtooth waves.

Change Rate → Tempo

Dynamic playback speed: baseline stable periods pace at 60 BPM; anomalous swings accelerate to 160 BPM.

Thresholds → Alarm

Crossing critical environmental limits fires a synchronized dissonant chime and high-contrast visual flash.

Story Persona

Each dataset uses tailored synthesis: AM Flute, FM Harmonic Drift, Crisis Distortion, Rainforest Rain Pad, or Wildfire Lead.

Longitude → Stereo Pan

Geographic longitude positions sound across stereo space: 180°W pans full left, prime meridian centers, and 180°E pans full right.
