REAL-WORLD SCIENTIFIC IMPACT

Turning Inaccessible Satellite Visuals into Decipherable Sound

NASA collects petabytes of Earth imagery every day. But 2.2 billion visually impaired individuals cannot inspect satellite heatmaps, and human researchers looking at hundreds of line charts suffer visual fatigue. Earth Jukebox solves both problems: human hearing has superior temporal acuity to the human eye, enabling rapid aural screening of climate anomalies, binaural era comparison, and fully accessible GIS science.

♿
1. BVI Earth Science Accessibility

Enables blind and low-vision scientists and students to explore NASA GIS datasets by ear, navigating multi-year climate trends via pitch intervals and tactile acoustic cursors.

🔍
2. Aural Anomaly Screening

The human ear can detect transient micro-deviations (2–5 ms) that visual chart-smoothing hides. Automates statistical outlier detection with instant acoustic sonar alarms.

⚖️
3. Binaural Era Comparator

Plays a historical baseline in the Left Ear and modern anomaly in the Right Ear. Harmonic consonance indicates stability; dissonance and acoustic beating reveal climate destabilization.

ACOUSTIC MAPPING METHODOLOGY
🎵 Value → Pitch

Monotonic linear mapping from $[min, max]$ to MIDI 48 (C3, 130.8 Hz) through MIDI 84 (C6, 1046.5 Hz).

🎻 Variability → Timbre

Uniform spatial data = pure sine wave; high spatial entropy = rich harmonic sawtooth wave.

⏱ Change Rate → Tempo

Dynamic playback pace: stable periods = 60 BPM; rapid anomalous swings = 160 BPM.

🚨 Thresholds → Alarm

Crossing critical limits triggers a layered bell chime + noise burst and screen flash.

🎙 Story Persona

Each story features dedicated synthesis: AM Flute · FM Drift · Crisis Distortion · Rain Pad · Wildfire Lead.

🌐 Longitude → Stereo Pan

Geographic coordinates pan audio from Left channel (−180°) to Right channel (+180°).
