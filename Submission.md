**Earth Jukebox** — Applied NASA Climate Sonification Workstation

## Project Summary (Short Description)
Earth Jukebox is an applied NASA Earth observation sonification workstation that translates multi-decadal satellite datasets into decipherable physical sound. Built natively in the browser without backend dependencies, it converts climate records from NASA POWER, VEDA, and GIBS into continuous audio using rigorous mathematical mappings: observed values dictate pitch (C3–C6), spatial variance shifts timbre, rate of change governs tempo, and longitude determines stereo pan.

The platform addresses the sonification challenge by transforming passive listening into an active research tool. It provides four specialized scientific instruments: a synchronized Mission Explorer with live GIBS satellite imagery, a Binaural Era Comparator that reveals decadal climate drift through harmonic acoustic beating, an automated 2σ Aural Anomaly Scanner, and an Accessible Earth Science Lab equipped with offline, on-device neural voice commands and Synaptics haptic scrubbing.

This work is critical because over 2.2 billion visually impaired individuals worldwide cannot inspect conventional visual heatmaps or satellite imagery. By leveraging human hearing’s superior temporal resolution over the eye, Earth Jukebox makes NASA climate science universally accessible to blind and low-vision researchers while enabling scientists to detect transient ecological shocks—such as the 2021 Pacific Northwest Heat Dome—faster than visual chart inspection. *(188 words)*
